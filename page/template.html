Outside-in indicator of AI R&D automation · 30 Sep 2026

Who writes Claude's prompt?

Anthropic's system prompts are public, through official release notes and leaks. Scoring only the text each version adds shows AI authorship of new prompt writing jumping from about 45% to about 89% in spring 2026. That is strong evidence the lab's own work crossed an automation threshold, and weak evidence about an intelligence explosion.

Why system prompts

The CASP/GovAI working paper What if automating AI R&D triggers an intelligence explosion? (Sept 2026) asks policymakers to track the fraction of research contributions produced by AI systems, and notes that almost all of that data sits inside the labs. System prompts are one of the few lab documents that are public, dated and rewritten with every release. Who writes them is a small, checkable proxy for how much of the lab's own work AI now does.

For each of 15 leaked Anthropic prompts I kept only the text appearing for the first time (absent from every earlier leak and every earlier official prompt), then scored it with the Pangram 4 AI detector. Carried-over text is never counted twice.

What this says about recursive self-improvement

ForSuggestive evidence

  • A sharp threshold. About 20 months at 30–55%, then a jump to 80–95% within two months of Opus 4.7. That is the pattern of a capability threshold being crossed for a task, which is how the paper expects automation to spread.
  • It matches the lab's self-reports. Anthropic reports AI wrote over 80% of approved code by May 2026 and completed 26% of R&D work autonomously by August. This outside measure breaks at the same time.
  • The rules that govern the model are now mostly model-written. Safety-policy text went from 46% to 82% AI and style text from 11% to 81%. Humans are moving from authors to reviewers, the oversight risk the paper flags.
  • Rewriting became cheap. Most existing human text was replaced within five months, and release gaps fell from a median of 73 to 22.5 days.

AgainstLimits and counter-evidence

  • No compounding before 2026. AI helped write about half the new text for nearly two years without the share rising. The 2026 jump is one step; an explosion needs the next steps to come faster.
  • Wrong part of the pipeline. System prompts are deployment configuration. Their authorship says nothing about returns to research effort, compute bottlenecks or capability growth.
  • Written is not decided. People may still specify and approve every line.
  • Cadence has other causes. Product tiers, point releases and competition also shorten release gaps, and core-prompt writing did not accelerate much.

Net: the data make "automation of lab work crossed a threshold in spring 2026" close to certain, and move the odds of a software intelligence explosion (versus fast automation without one) only slightly upward. The discriminating observation is whether the next jump arrives sooner than this one did.

Method and caveats

  • Data. 21 dated official claude.ai prompts (Anthropic release notes, Jul 2024–Sep 2026) and 15 leaked prompts from the CL4R1T4S repository. Leaks reproduce official punctuation exactly on every checkable sentence (59 of 59). The Opus 5.5 leak is a full agent-app session; only its system prompt, skills and a sample of reference files were scored.
  • First-appearance text. A line counts as new if at least half its words sit in sentences never seen before, with model names and dates normalised. About 139k words were scored.
  • Detector. Pangram 4, segment-level labels mapped back to lines and prompt sections. There was no human-written technical-documentation control, so early tool-docs figures may include false positives. The change over time is robust: same detector, and core sections moved from 0–11% to about 80%.
  • Coverage. Leaks are snapshots of different products (claude.ai, Claude Design, the agent app), and some releases have no leak.

Sources: Anthropic system prompt release notes · CL4R1T4S leaked prompts · Pangram · Chan, Mindermann et al., What if automating AI R&D triggers an intelligence explosion?, Frontier AI Working Paper 2/2026.
